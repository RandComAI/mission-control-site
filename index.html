More agents are easy. Efficient agents are hard.

Use the team you need—not the context you don’t.

Anyone can spawn an army of agents. The hidden trap is token cost: every session reads context, long conversations keep growing, and coordination adds more. RandComp Studio keeps durable work outside the conversation, starts focused sessions for the task, and connects them only when collaboration is useful.

Follow one real workflow

One task. Three ways to stay in control.

Start on your Mac, leave the desk, and keep directing the same work from another AI session. RandComp does not create three copies of the task.

Example goal: “Launch a small online store. Have Product choose the scope, Developer build it, and QA verify it.”

1

At your Mac

See the whole task in the desktop app.

Start with an outcome, approve the roles, and watch the task tree form. Every node tells you what that agent is doing, what it is waiting for, and what it produced. Select one to read or redirect its work.

Desktop app · visual control
2

Away from the desk

Check the same team over SSH.

The sessions continue on the remote computer. Use the lightweight CLI to see what needs you, read an agent, answer a question, or attach directly—without streaming the desktop.

CLI · low-bandwidth remote control
ssh studio-mac · ~/store

$ randcomp status

Online store · 3 working · 1 needs you

Developer asks: reuse the existing checkout design?

$ randcomp agent send developer-2 "Yes—reuse it."

Sent to Developer.

The task, team, messages, and results are the same ones shown in the desktop app.
3

Inside your AI session

Let Codex operate RandComp through MCP.

Keep talking naturally to your AI session. Ask it to check RandComp, summarize the team, or pass a decision to an agent. MCP connects that conversation to the same local task underneath.

Review one collected outcome.

Long-lived work, short-lived context

Resume the role—not an enormous conversation.

The durable state is ordinary Markdown: the role’s instructions, task goal, decisions, messages, handoffs, and results. Stop a session when its job is done. A later session—or another supported CLI—can resume from the saved work without inheriting every old token.
